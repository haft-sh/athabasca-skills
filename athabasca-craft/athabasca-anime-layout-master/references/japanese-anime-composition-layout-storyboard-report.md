## Practical Synthesis

### Core idea
A layout should begin from the character's emotional position in the world, not from decorative beauty. Composition is psychology, geography, and rhythm inside a rectangle.

### High-value principles
- Prioritize readable silhouettes before detail.
- Make gesture read before facial micro-detail.
- Control the viewer's first read, then the secondary and tertiary reads.
- Use foreground, midground, and background to create pressure, scale, and spatial clarity.
- Treat horizon line and vanishing point as emotional tools, not merely technical perspective markers.
- Use negative space to imply absence, waiting, threat, or uncertainty.
- Avoid default centering unless authority, ritual, confrontation, or irony justify it.
- Different genres want different compositional defaults: fantasy favors grandeur and verticality; slice of life favors eye level and naturalism; horror favors off-screen tension and invasive space.

### Vanishing point logic
- Inside frame: destination, control, ritual, clear read.
- Outside frame: natural world, continuation, off-screen tension.
- Near edge: distortion, pressure, speed.
- Very distant: calm, maturity, softness.

### Horizon logic
- Visible horizon: clear geography.
- High horizon / more ground: defeat, vulnerability, sinking.
- Low horizon / more sky: heroism, threat, grandeur.
- Horizon outside above frame: high camera, surveillance, cold observation.
- Horizon outside below frame: low camera, monumentality, vertical dominance.
- Invisible but implied horizon: natural interior drama and slice-of-life realism.

When designing or critiquing shots, address as needed:

0. Core idea
A layout does not begin by asking:

"What shot looks beautiful?"
It begins by asking:

"Where is the character emotionally within the world?"
Then you decide:
• Is the world crushing them?
• Does the character dominate?
• Are they alone?
• Are they in conflict?
• Are they in motion?
• Does the camera accompany them, judge them, or threaten them?
• Is the space intimate, epic, everyday, oppressive, or mysterious?
• Should the viewer look at the face, the hand, the object, the background, or the emptiness?
Composition is psychology, geography, and rhythm inside a rectangle.
