# Short Film and Micro-Story Playbook

## Core principle

A short film is not a small feature. It is usually one charged situation, one conflict, one decisive turn, and one resonant aftertaste.

## Best use cases for short format

Shorts excel at:

- a single moral choice
- a ritual interrupted
- a secret revealed
- a relationship changed
- a flawed habit exposed
- a visual metaphor completed
- a moment before or after a larger unseen story
- a joke with emotional truth
- a child learning one repeatable emotional skill

## Short-film constraints

For 2–3 minutes, assume:

- one protagonist
- one primary conflict
- one or two locations
- minimal backstory
- one emotional arc
- one clear ending image

For 5–10 minutes, you can add:

- one supporting relationship
- a slightly more complex reversal
- a stronger midpoint turn
- one subplot only if it directly sharpens the main story

## Common short-film failures

- feature-sized premise squeezed into too little time
- lore instead of drama
- too many characters
- no decisive turn
- twist ending without emotional setup
- voiceover explaining what images should dramatize
- “mood piece” with no active pressure
- moral message without dramatic test

## 2–3 minute beat template

### 0:00–0:20 Hook
Open with pressure, contrast, image, or behavior. Do not start with abstract explanation.

### 0:20–0:45 Normal strategy
Show how the protagonist usually copes or performs.

### 0:45–1:20 Disruption
The strategy fails. A problem becomes unavoidable.

### 1:20–1:55 Cost / recognition
The protagonist sees or feels the cost of the old strategy.

### 1:55–2:30 New action
They attempt a different behavior.

### 2:30–3:00 Changed image / button
A concise final image proves the emotional change.

## Short-film scoring emphasis

For shorts, weight these heavily:

- clarity of premise
- compression
- immediacy
- visual storytelling
- decisive turn
- ending image
- filmability

## Rewrite moves for shorts

- Combine characters.
- Combine locations.
- Start later.
- Remove backstory.
- Externalize the internal conflict with a task, rule, deadline, or prop.
- Make the ending mirror the opening.
- Replace explanation with a repeated action that changes meaning.
