# Source Bibliography and Research Notes

This skill synthesizes common professional screenwriting and children’s-media development principles. It does not copy any single source and should not present itself as a replacement for the original books or courses.

## Major screenwriting / dramaturgy influences

- Robert McKee, *Story*: inciting incident, object of desire, forces of antagonism, progressive complications, crisis/climax/resolution, story values.
- Syd Field, *Screenplay*: three-act paradigm, setup/confrontation/resolution, act turns.
- Christopher Vogler, *The Writer’s Journey*: practical adaptation of mythic Hero’s Journey stages and archetypes for screenwriters.
- Joseph Campbell, *The Hero with a Thousand Faces*: monomyth and mythic transformation pattern.
- John Truby, *The Anatomy of Story*: weakness/need/desire, moral argument, opponent, self-revelation, story world, symbol web.
- Lajos Egri, *The Art of Dramatic Writing*: premise, character contradiction, conflict.
- Aristotle, *Poetics*: action, causality, reversal, recognition, catharsis.
- David Mamet / practical scene-writing traditions: objective, obstacle, tactic, behavior over explanation.

## Short-film development influences

- Sundance Collab short-film screenplay curriculum topics: story structure, logline, protagonist wants and needs, dramatic problem, conflict, scene craft, behavior/action/dialogue, openings/endings, visual storytelling.
- Raindance short-film guidance: shorts often work best as a single moment with conflict, dilemma, choice, and sometimes a ticking clock; short films under roughly ten minutes should generally focus on one major idea and one major conflict.

## Children’s / family media influences

- Sesame Workshop public SEL materials: naming emotions, understanding facial/body cues, managing emotions, practicing self-regulation strategies, empathy, resilience, friendship, calming strategies.
- Common children’s television development practice: repetition with variation, concrete stakes, emotionally legible behavior, age-appropriate humor, safety, reassurance, and repeatable takeaways.

## Practical synthesis

The skill converts these influences into an agent-usable diagnostic and repair framework:

- identify dramatic engine
- score applicable craft categories
- locate structural failure points
- recommend prioritized fixes
- produce alternate creative solutions
- adapt notes for short-film and children’s episode constraints
