# Short-Form Growth and Distribution Rubric

Use this rubric for concepts, drafts, and published posts. It scores the **quality of the creative hypothesis**, not predicted view count.

## 100-point scorecard

| Dimension | Weight | Core question |
|---|---:|---|
| 1. Stop power and immediate clarity | 15 | Would the right stranger stop and understand why? |
| 2. Hold, progression, and completion | 15 | Does each beat/card create forward pressure? |
| 3. Payoff and trust | 10 | Does the ending satisfy or exceed the opening contract? |
| 4. Share, send, and save motivation | 12 | Why would someone transmit or keep this? |
| 5. Participation and comment quality | 10 | Is there an intrinsic, low-friction reason to respond? |
| 6. Audience-product fit | 10 | Will likely engagers also care about the promoted thing? |
| 7. Follow and series potential | 10 | Is the recurring account promise concrete and desirable? |
| 8. Conversion bridge | 8 | Is the next action natural, visible, and proportionate? |
| 9. Platform-native execution | 6 | Does the format exploit native behavior and constraints? |
| 10. Distinctiveness, feasibility, and integrity | 4 | Is it ownable, repeatable, producible, and trustworthy? |
| **Total** | **100** | |

## Scoring anchors

For each dimension, use the percentage anchors below, then multiply by its weight.

- **0% — absent or counterproductive**
- **25% — weak, vague, or dependent on luck**
- **50% — functional but ordinary**
- **75% — strong and specific**
- **100% — exceptional, unusually clear, and hard to improve without audience evidence**

Use intermediate values when warranted. Explain the score briefly.

## Dimension details

### 1. Stop power and immediate clarity — 15

Evaluate:

- first frame/first sentence,
- visual legibility on a phone,
- relevance to a cold viewer,
- specificity,
- novelty or tension,
- and whether the premise stands alone without account context.

**Full-score behavior:** the intended viewer recognizes the topic and feels a concrete reason to continue almost instantly.

**Common deductions:** setup before value, tiny text, generic question, brand-first opening, unclear subject, context that only followers understand.

### 2. Hold, progression, and completion — 15

Evaluate:

- escalation,
- card-to-card swipe pressure,
- changing visual states,
- fair delay of closure,
- information density,
- and absence of redundant beats.

**Full-score behavior:** removing any beat would damage the progression; every beat changes what the viewer knows or expects.

**Common deductions:** five interchangeable cards, answer obvious too early, static pacing, repetitive examples, premature CTA.

### 3. Payoff and trust — 10

Evaluate:

- whether the promise is fulfilled,
- reveal quality,
- emotional or informational satisfaction,
- replay/loop possibility,
- and whether a click is required merely to receive the promised value.

**Full-score behavior:** closure feels earned and makes the account more trustworthy.

**Common deductions:** no answer, answer hidden off-platform, weak reveal, bait-and-switch, CTA substituted for payoff.

### 4. Share, send, and save motivation — 12

Evaluate explicit transmission motives:

- identity,
- social currency,
- utility,
- emotion,
- connection,
- comparison,
- or replay/reference value.

**Full-score behavior:** a viewer can immediately name a specific person or social context for sharing/saving.

**Common deductions:** merely likable; too branded; no recipient; disposable entertainment with no identity or utility.

### 5. Participation and comment quality — 10

Evaluate:

- clarity of response task,
- friction,
- room for meaningful answers,
- social proof/debate,
- ability to compare scores,
- creator reply potential,
- and resistance to low-quality “yes/no” bait.

**Full-score behavior:** participation is part of the entertainment and produces comments worth reading or replying to.

**Common deductions:** “thoughts?”, too many requested answers, impossible challenge, answer requires leaving the app, or generic “comment below.”

### 6. Audience-product fit — 10

Evaluate:

- overlap between people likely to engage and people likely to want the offer,
- qualification strength,
- demonstrated use case,
- and whether broad reach dilutes relevance.

**Full-score behavior:** the content’s entertainment/utility is generated by the same desire the product serves.

**Common deductions:** unrelated meme reach, trend bolted onto product, audience too broad, unclear benefit.

### 7. Follow and series potential — 10

Evaluate:

- recognizable recurring format,
- specificity of future value,
- variation capacity,
- cadence sustainability,
- anticipation,
- and whether the next installment can become meaningfully different or harder.

**Full-score behavior:** viewers can predict the category of future reward without predicting the exact content.

**Common deductions:** one-off stunt, repetitive series fatigue, generic “daily challenge,” no escalating or rotating structure.

### 8. Conversion bridge — 8

Evaluate:

- naturalness of product role,
- CTA timing,
- clarity of next action,
- continuity between in-feed experience and destination,
- and friction proportional to viewer intent.

**Full-score behavior:** the product is the obvious way to continue or obtain the demonstrated value.

**Common deductions:** abrupt sales pitch, homepage with no continuation, multiple CTAs, link request before payoff, hidden product relevance.

### 9. Platform-native execution — 6

Evaluate:

- correct native format,
- vertical/mobile layout,
- safe zones,
- readable captions,
- sound role,
- original content,
- native features/remixes when relevant,
- and no cross-platform watermark.

**Full-score behavior:** the idea would lose something if mechanically transplanted unchanged to another platform.

### 10. Distinctiveness, feasibility, and integrity — 4

Evaluate:

- ownable angle,
- production repeatability,
- asset/legal safety,
- honest promise,
- and absence of obvious plagiarism or manipulation.

## Score caps and fatal flaws

Apply caps after calculating the raw score. Explain the cap.

| Flaw | Maximum total |
|---|---:|
| Viewer cannot identify topic or task in the opening | 59 |
| Hook promises a payoff the post never supplies | 49 |
| Content only works as an advertisement, with little intrinsic viewer value | 54 |
| Product/offer is unrelated to the audience attracted | 59 |
| Core participation requires excessive effort or off-platform action | 64 |
| Material deception, fake proof, rights violation, dangerous participation, or plagiarism | 0 until repaired |

A concept may score below the cap naturally; never raise it to the cap.

## Confidence label

Always attach confidence:

- **Low:** premise only; audience, creative, or execution unknown.
- **Medium:** full draft/assets available or based on repeated account patterns.
- **High:** post-level analytics plus comparable account history are available.

Even “high confidence” is diagnostic, not certainty about future distribution.

## Objective-specific overlays

The base total stays comparable, but call out the objective lens.

### Reach-first

Prioritize dimensions 1–5. Warn if reach is poorly qualified.

### Follow-growth-first

Prioritize dimensions 1, 4, 6, and 7. A high-view concept with no account promise is not a growth winner.

### Traffic/conversion-first

Prioritize dimensions 2, 3, 6, and 8. Do not sacrifice trust to inflate clicks.

### Community-first

Prioritize dimensions 4, 5, and 7. Comment quality matters more than raw count.

## Post-publish rescoring

Do not mechanically convert analytics into the creative score. Instead:

1. Preserve the pre-publish hypothesis score.
2. Record observed funnel data.
3. Mark each diagnosis as confirmed, rejected, or unresolved.
4. Rescore only where evidence changes the creative assessment.
5. Document the next variant and the single variable changed.

This preserves learning instead of rewriting the original prediction after the fact.
