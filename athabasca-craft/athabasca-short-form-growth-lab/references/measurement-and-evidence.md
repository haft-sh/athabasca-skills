# Measurement and Evidence

## Evidence hierarchy

Use sources in this order for current claims:

1. Official platform product, creator, transparency, recommendation, and policy documentation.
2. The account’s own post-level analytics and comparable history.
3. Independent peer-reviewed behavioral or communication research.
4. Transparent third-party datasets with disclosed method and sample.
5. Creator anecdotes and platform case studies as hypotheses only.

Never present a secret-algorithm claim as fact. Exact ranking weights are not public and change.

## Durable platform observations

### Instagram

Instagram stated in February 2025 that Reel recommendation signals include watch time, retention, shares, likes, and comments. It described a staged non-follower distribution process: a Reel is first shown to a small likely audience; stronger performers may be shown to progressively wider groups. It also advised:

- content that stands alone for non-followers,
- a compelling hook in the first three seconds,
- monitoring three-second View Rate,
- relatable/shareable ideas,
- regular posting,
- original/recommendation-eligible content,
- and experimentation, including Trial Reels.

The same official article reported more than 4.5 billion Reel reshares per day across Instagram and Facebook at that time. This supports treating sends/shares as a first-class creative objective, but it does not reveal a universal ranking formula.

**Source:** Instagram for Creators, “Helping creators of all sizes break through,” February 26, 2025.
https://creators.instagram.com/blog/helping-creators-of-all-sizes-break-through

Instagram’s Trial Reels announcement says eligible creators can show a Reel to non-followers first, inspect views/likes/comments/shares after roughly 24 hours, and optionally share it more broadly; automatic follower sharing can be based on early performance within 72 hours. Features and eligibility may change, so verify in-app/current docs.

**Source:** Instagram for Creators, “Trial reels,” December 10, 2024.
https://creators.instagram.com/blog/instagram-trial-reels

Instagram also extends originality guidance across Reels, photos, and carousels. Verify current eligibility/policy details before making compliance claims.

**Sources:**
https://creators.instagram.com/original-content-guidelines
https://creators.instagram.com/blog/rewarding-original-creators-on-instagram

### TikTok

TikTok’s public recommendation explainer identifies:

- user interactions such as likes, shares, follows, comments, and created content,
- video information such as captions, sounds, and hashtags,
- and lower-weight account/device settings.

It explicitly gives finishing a longer video as an example of a stronger interest signal than same-country device context, and says follower count and previous high-performing videos are not direct recommendation factors. It also says duplicated/spam content can be ineligible or disfavored.

**Source:** TikTok Newsroom, “How TikTok recommends videos #ForYou,” June 18, 2020. This is an older but still useful public explanation; verify newer product/policy details for current decisions.
https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/how-tiktok-recommends-videos-for-you

TikTok For Business recommends TikTok-native execution, vertical high-resolution production, safe-space awareness, text overlays, hook/body/close structure, stimulation through editing, and sound. Its Creative Codes evidence is primarily advertising research, so treat it as directional for organic creative rather than proof of organic ranking.

**Source:** TikTok For Business, “Creative Codes: 6 principles for creating on TikTok.”
https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en/blog/creative-best-practices-top-performing-ads

TikTok’s short-video guide emphasizes the first few seconds, clear product value, and a clear CTA. Again, it is commerce/ad guidance rather than a neutral organic-ranking disclosure.

**Source:** TikTok For Business, “Making TikTok videos for high engagement,” November 24, 2024.
https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en/blog/tiktok-short-video-best-practice

## Behavioral principles

### Curiosity and information gaps

Loewenstein’s information-gap account frames curiosity as attention to a gap between what a person knows and wants to know. For creative work, the practical implication is not “hide everything.” Show enough context that the gap becomes specific and resolvable. A gap that is too large or vague can produce indifference rather than curiosity.

**Source:** “The Psychology of Curiosity: A Review and Reinterpretation,” 1994.
https://www.cmu.edu/dietrich/sds/docs/loewenstein/PsychofCuriosity.pdf

### Emotion and sharing

Berger and Milkman found that online sharing is associated not simply with positive versus negative valence, but with arousal: high-arousal emotions such as awe, anger, and anxiety were more associated with virality than low-arousal states such as sadness. Do not turn this into a mandate for outrage. Awe, delight, tension, and energized recognition can produce arousal without damaging trust.

**Source:** Jonah Berger and Katherine Milkman, “What Makes Online Content Viral?”, Journal of Marketing Research, 2012.
https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1509/jmr.10.0353

## Measurement funnel

Choose metrics based on the objective and format. Metric names vary by platform and account type.

### Distribution

- impressions,
- reach/unique viewers,
- percentage from non-followers or recommendation surfaces,
- views per impression when available.

### Initial attention

- first-second or first-three-second survival/view rate,
- swipe-away rate,
- early retention curve.

### Hold and payoff

- average watch time,
- average percentage viewed,
- completion rate,
- replay rate or views per viewer,
- carousel reach beyond first card / swipe depth when available.

### Transmission and value

- shares/sends per reached viewer,
- saves per reached viewer,
- meaningful comments per reached viewer,
- likes as a lower-commitment supporting signal.

### Growth

- profile visits per reached non-follower,
- follows per profile visit,
- follows per reached non-follower,
- repeat viewers when available.

### Conversion

- link clicks per profile visit,
- landing-page sessions,
- sign-up/purchase conversion,
- qualified conversion,
- downstream retention or revenue.

A high click rate into a poor landing experience is not a content victory. Diagnose the full path.

## Diagnostic map

| Symptom | Likely leak | First test |
|---|---|---|
| Reach occurs, immediate drop | hook clarity/relevance | new first frame or opening line |
| Good early hold, weak completion | repetitive body or delayed payoff | remove/combine middle beats |
| Good completion, few shares/saves | weak transmission motive | add identity, utility, comparison, or recipient context |
| Many comments, few follows | participation unrelated to account promise | make next episode/series value explicit |
| Many views, few profile visits | product/account relevance invisible | integrate account-specific mechanism earlier |
| Profile visits, few follows | weak profile or future-value promise | align bio/pinned posts/series naming |
| Good follows, few clicks | CTA or destination discontinuity | make destination continue the in-feed experience |
| Good clicks, poor conversion | landing/offer mismatch | repair destination, not the Reel |

These are hypotheses. Check the retention curve, comments, traffic quality, and destination data before concluding.

## Account-relative analysis

Prefer the account’s own baseline.

For each format, maintain recent medians for:

- reach,
- non-follower reach,
- three-second view rate or early hold,
- average percentage viewed,
- completion,
- shares per reach,
- saves per reach,
- comments per reach,
- profile visits per reach,
- follows per profile visit,
- clicks per profile visit,
- and conversions per click.

Compare like with like:

- same format,
- similar duration/card count,
- similar topic/audience,
- organic versus paid separated,
- and enough time since posting.

Do not compare a seven-second meme directly with a 45-second tutorial and declare the longer post a failure because completion is lower.

## Experiment design

### Pre-publish record

For every serious test, save:

- hypothesis,
- objective,
- intended audience,
- concept and viewer contract,
- primary metric,
- diagnostic metric,
- variable changed,
- expected direction,
- and stopping/iteration rule.

### Variant discipline

Change one major variable where possible:

- first frame/hook,
- body length,
- difficulty,
- payoff timing,
- format,
- host presence,
- CTA,
- or audience framing.

Platform distribution is noisy and not a laboratory. “One variable” improves learning but does not eliminate confounds.

### Decision language

Use:

- **Promote/continue:** repeated evidence above account baseline on the objective metric.
- **Iterate:** promising signal with a diagnosable funnel leak.
- **Retire:** repeated weak performance after meaningful repairs, or poor audience-product fit.
- **Unresolved:** sample too small/noisy or analytics incomplete.

Avoid declaring a winner from one post unless the effect is very large and strategically coherent.

## Source refresh procedure

When asked about a current feature, policy, or algorithm:

1. Search official Instagram/Meta and TikTok sources first.
2. Record publication/update date and region/account eligibility.
3. Separate feed/recommendation guidance from ad-performance research.
4. Verify feature availability in current documentation or in-app if relevant.
5. Update this reference if a durable claim has materially changed.
6. Do not preserve stale numeric benchmarks as timeless truths.
